## Reasons

### Requirements stability
How well does each approach handle how clear or changeable our requirements are?

- Waterfall: Requirements are frozen after weeks 5-6, so changes such as "seats left" in the event list would be costly.
- Incremental: Each increment can absorb new requirements, though the overall plan stays fairly fixed.
- Scrum-style: The backlog is reprioritised every sprint, so changing requirements are expected.

### Client availability
Does each approach suit how often we can get client feedback?

- Waterfall: The client is needed at the start and end, which wastes the weekly 30-minute slot in between.
- Incremental: Feedback after each increment fits a TA who is available weekly.
- Scrum-style: A sprint review with the TA every two weeks matches the available time.

### Team experience
How well could our team follow each approach?

- Waterfall: A simple linear plan is easy for a student team to follow.
- Incremental: We have built small parts in earlier courses, but planning increments is less familiar.
- Scrum-style: Roles and ceremonies are new to us, but the rotating Scrum Master helps us learn.

### Deadline (week 14)
How likely is each approach to give us something working to demonstrate?

- Waterfall: Integration and testing come last, so a failure leaves nothing to demo.
- Incremental: A working core exists after the first increment (week 10).
- Scrum-style: Two sprints (weeks 9-12) plus week 13 hardening make a working demo very likely.

### Risk
How well would each approach help us find misunderstood rules,
bugs and problems connecting our code early?

- Waterfall: Rule bugs and integration problems appear only at the end.
- Incremental: Each increment is tested, so problems surface earlier.
- Scrum-style: Tests run every sprint against the capacity, duplicate and 24 h rules, so issues surface early.
