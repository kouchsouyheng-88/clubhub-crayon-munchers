## Reasons

### Requirements stability
- Waterfall: Fits badly. It needs the requirements fixed before design starts, but ours are still vague in week 4 and the client will change them after each review.
- Incremental: Fits partly. Each increment can be refined, but the overall scope is mostly decided up front, so a big change is awkward.
- Scrum-style: Fits well. Changes go into the product backlog and are planned at the next sprint planning.

### Client availability
- Waterfall: The client is needed mainly at the start and the end, so the 30 minutes a week is wasted in between and we only learn about mistakes at the end.
- Incremental: Feedback arrives when an increment is delivered, which is better, but the timing is not fixed.
- Scrum-style: A review every two weeks matches the 30 minutes a week we can get from the TA.

### Team experience
- Waterfall: We know plan-driven work from other courses, so it is the easiest to follow.
- Incremental: A small step from plan-driven work, so we can follow it with little new learning.
- Scrum-style: Sprints, planning and retrospectives are new to all of us, so the first sprint will be slow while we learn the roles and meetings.

### Deadline (week 14)
- Waterfall: Testing happens at the end, so if anything slips there may be nothing working to demo in week 14.
- Incremental: A working core, such as browse and register, exists after the first increment.
- Scrum-style: Sprint 1 in weeks 9-10 should already give a demoable core, with Sprint 2 and the hardening week to improve it.

### Risk
- Waterfall: Misunderstood rules such as capacity, duplicates and the 24 hour cancellation are found at the end, when they are expensive to fix.
- Incremental: Problems are found per increment, but only when an increment is finished and tested.
- Scrum-style: Pull requests with tests and a review every two weeks show wrong rules and integration problems early.
